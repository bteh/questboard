# The payment constitution

One rule, stated once, so nobody ever has to wonder:

**Questboard never accepts payment to list, rank, recommend, or grant
access to people. Listed parties never pay. Seekers may, whether they
are a person or a studio.**

What that means in practice:

- No employer, grant maker, conference, accelerator, research firm,
  bank, or casting company can pay to be on the board, to rank higher
  on it, or to reach the people using it. There is no sales channel for
  this; the answer is no before the email is opened.
- Every quest is pinned because it cleared the same bars as every other
  quest (real ask, real place to act, real counterparty, real date,
  pay only as stated), never because someone paid.
- The board itself is free, forever, for everyone.
- The only money Questboard ever takes is from the SEEKER side, for
  depth on top of the free board (one-time or seasonal passes, never
  subscriptions dressed as passes, never access to listings). A studio
  hunting clients is a seeker; a conference hunting speakers is a
  listed party. The role decides, not the company size.
- Ranking is fit and freshness. There is no boost product, and there
  never will be one.

Why this is load-bearing: a board that takes listing money eventually
shows you what paid to be shown. The entire product is the promise that
what you see is what is real. Selling placement would spend the only
thing Questboard has.

Adopted 2026-07-10 (the strategy council's product-writer seat, adopted
verbatim by all five seats); published with the B2B pilot.

## Addendum: affiliate on side-quest on-ramps (2026-07-14)

The rule above still holds without exception on the job lane and on
ranking. This addendum names the one narrow place money may come from a
listed party, and the mechanism that keeps it from ever buying position.

**The narrowing, stated once: Questboard may earn a standard affiliate
bounty when a person, on their own, signs up for the platform a
side-quest runs on. Never on the job lane. Never in a way that can change
what is shown or the order it is shown in.**

Why this is not a boost product. The constitution bans payment "to list,
rank, recommend, or grant access." An affiliate bounty under the rule
below does none of those: the platform earns its place on merit alone,
the person picks it freely, and the bounty is collected only after that
free choice. The money rides the person's success. It does not buy the
person's attention.

The bright line between a good affiliate and employer money: **money is
allowed only when the payer profits from the person succeeding, not from
the person merely showing up.** A resale marketplace makes money when you
actually sell. A gig platform that pays per driver signed up profits from
throughput of bodies whether or not the work is worth anyone's time; that
is employer money wearing a side-quest costume, and it is banned. When in
doubt, ask who the payer wins from: the person's outcome, or the person's
arrival.

### Lanes

- **Allowed now:** resale (eBay Partner Network, Poshmark, Whatnot),
  rent-your-car (Turo, with the wear/insurance/depreciation catch shown),
  and bank *deposit-account* bonuses (no credit pull, simple catch).
- **Allowed with care, not first:** pet and house sitting (Rover,
  Care.com), noting that a platform charging the sitter to access unpaid
  work is not allowed. Credit-card bonuses come last of all, only after
  the pay-blind ranking below is live and proven, because the highest
  bounties sit on the worst cards and the compliance load (Reg Z / TILA)
  is a standing burden. Never quote a card's own terms; link to the
  issuer.
- **Never, by policy:** the job lane; gig-driver / task-app signup CPA
  (pays per body); clinical-trial and plasma recruitment (per-subject
  finder's fees are an ethics line, not a risk to price in); anything
  that re-gambles the bank-&-card lane.

### The guardrails that make it real (all required)

1. **Pay-blind ranking, enforced in code.** The bounty is a display
   field, never an input to the sort. This is proven by a test that
   asserts the ranking is identical with every bounty zeroed and with the
   real bounties in place. That test is this promise, mechanized; without
   it the promise is just a vow.
2. **The non-paying option always appears in its true position,** labeled
   plainly ("we earn nothing if you pick this"), including "do it
   yourself / don't get this" as a rankable first place.
3. **A negative-EV or predatory option is dropped regardless of payout,**
   on published criteria.
4. **The catch is shown at equal or greater weight than the bounty,** as
   a render gate: no monetized row ships without its catch.
5. **Disclosure sits at each recommendation** (FTC clear-and-conspicuous),
   plus one plain "how we make money" page.
6. **This lane list is the allowlist.** Adding a lane is a constitution
   edit, not a product decision.
7. **Affiliate stays supplementary.** Free core plus the seeker pass is
   the anchor. If affiliate ever crosses a set share of revenue, that is
   a governance flag, because a board that needs the conversion starts
   bending toward it.

Kill conditions: if the pay-blind ranking or the true-position rule
cannot hold on a lane, that lane earns nothing. First lane to build:
resale, the lowest-stakes place to prove the machinery.

Adopted 2026-07-14 after an octo:council pressure-test (strategy,
security, and code-review seats all returned "adopt, but revise";
resale-first and the pay-blind sort test are theirs).

## Second addendum: one labeled sponsor (2026-07-15)

The original rule said "no ads, ever." That was really two rules wearing
one word: no money may move what ranks or what gets on the board (keep
this forever), and no annoying ad-network junk (keep this too). This
addendum keeps both and opens one narrow, honest door.

**The narrowing, stated once: the digest and a lane may each carry a
single clearly labeled sponsor. A sponsor buys a spot to say hello, never
a rank, never inclusion of a listing, never a quest.**

Adopted. Kept banned.

- **Adopt:** the pay-blind affiliate model (already live); one labeled
  sponsor on the digest; one "presented by" patron per lane, a neutral
  party only.
- **Stay banned, forever:** all programmatic display, banner, and
  ad-network units; pop-ups, interstitials, autoplay, sticky/anchored
  units; retargeting and any third-party tracking pixel; pay-to-rank or
  pay-for-placement of any listing; employer or counterparty money that
  buys a row's inclusion or position; sponsored quests (a paid posting in
  the ranked set). These are the moat.

The guardrails, written so they bind:

1. **Labeled, unmissable.** Every paid element wears a fixed label
   ("Sponsor", "Paid link", "Presented by"), adjacent to the item and
   visible at the same time, never behind a hover (FTC clear-and-
   conspicuous; "paid link", not "affiliate link").
2. **Never in the ranked set.** A sponsor sits in a fixed labeled slot
   outside the sorted rows and never interleaves with them. The sort
   engine cannot read a sponsor or a payout field.
3. **Same actionability bar.** Nothing paid lowers the row contract; a
   sponsor is not a quest and is not counted as one.
4. **One per surface.** At most one sponsor visible at once (one on the
   digest, one per lane). The density cap is the "not annoying" contract.
5. **No counterparty conflict.** A sponsor may never be the counterparty
   of a listing it sits beside; prefer neutral tools over parties who
   benefit from being listed.
6. **Killable.** A global switch removes every paid element with zero
   product regression. Nothing structural depends on one advertiser.

The line that protects the whole board: money may attach to an outcome
you wanted, but it may never move what ranks or what gets in.

Adopted 2026-07-15. Scope confirmed by the owner (affiliate plus one
labeled sponsor; display advertising stays banned).
