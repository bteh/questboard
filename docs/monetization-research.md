# Monetization research: how Questboard earns without breaking its own rules

Logged 2026-07-09. Source: 4-agent workflow (Reddit WTP sweeps on career tools + gig-side, web
verification of ~17 competitor pricing pages, offer-moment/outcome-model analysis). Constraints
this was designed within (already decided, not re-litigated): free tier forever, browsing never
needs an account, no ads, no employer/poster money, one-time purchases over subscriptions.

---

## The blunt headline

Nobody in this audience volunteers to pay. Zero "I'd happily pay $X" statements surfaced on the
career side; the signal is refusal, workaround-seeking, and regret. People buy gray-market LinkedIn
Premium vouchers for ~$25/yr instead of paying list, and the beermoney crowd states the reflex
plainly: "I refuse to pay money into apps in general, but especially for an app that's supposed to
be giving me money." Two gig drivers are independently building their own trackers and marketing
"zero monthly subscriptions" as the headline feature.

The one narrow place wallets open: a concrete, deadline-bound, dollar-denominated payback at the
exact moment of need. Everlance at $9/mo justified purely by tax-season savings ("saved my ass a
couple hundred bucks come tax season"). Solo's $0.25-0.40 pay-guarantee credit bought per shift.
Rora's success fee charged only when the offer lands. Levels.fyi's flat $660-$1,887 with a
money-back floor. Pay-at-the-moment-of-concrete-value is the entire game.

## Model verdicts (from real pricing pages + real complaints)

| Model | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Subscription (Teal $29/mo, Huntr $40/mo, JobScan $49.95/mo, Simplify $19.99/wk) | Resented category-wide | universal "worth it as a short sprint, not a rolling cost"; cancel-seeking threads; "I paid for a Huntr subscription; I should probably use it. 😳" |
| Pay-to-apply while marketing "free" | Toxic + legal exposure | Backstage $150/yr to submit; Casting Networks facing a CA class action over exactly this |
| Metered per-action fees | Most complaint-generating shape found | Actors Access $22/minute video fee; $44 charged for an 83-second clip |
| Take-rate on worker payout | Reads as a tax on earned money; needs payment custody we don't have | Airtasker ~17% blended take; TaskRabbit fees |
| Tip jar / honor model | ~2% conversion ceiling at best-in-class trust (Wikipedia); rounding error | no honor-model vendor publishes conversion, which says it all |
| Free-for-user, employer pays | Proven (Otta: "no premium tiers for candidates") | ruled out by our constraints; also the User Interviews failure mode: the paying side can void the worker's pay |
| Success fee at the offer moment | The one clean, can't-bait-and-switch model | Rora "no increase, no fee"; only works where a negotiable number + big dollar swing exists = careers only |
| One-time pass, free core | Fits every constraint; matches the only WTP shape found | Simplify's feature split (minus its weekly meter); Solo's moment-of-need micro-purchase UX |

## The recommendation

1. **The whole board stays free, loudly.** Browse, search, filter, clip, log: free forever, no
   account to browse. Say "free forever, no subscription" as an active marketing claim; in this
   scam-scarred audience the absence of a meter is a feature people literally build products around.
2. **Offer Pass (career kind only).** Flat one-time purchase offered at the single real negotiable
   moment: when a user logs a job offer in Your Log. Comp benchmark + negotiation script bundle,
   Levels.fyi-shaped with a plain money-back line. Flat fee, never a percentage (a cut of pay reads
   extractive at gig scale, and % fights the pass model). Never offered on gig kinds: focus groups,
   casting, refereeing have NO negotiation moment (pay is fixed by the poster), so manufacturing an
   "offer moment" there would be false advertising against an honesty brand.
3. **Search Pass carries the volume.** One-time unlock, per active search season, of the deeper
   conveniences (AI tailoring depth, richer fit, saved-search alerts). Framing rule from the data:
   it must never look like "pay to see more quests" or "pay to apply"; the free board is complete.
   The pass buys depth on top, not access.
4. **Optional supporter add-on, Obsidian-style.** Zero feature gate, budget near-zero revenue.
5. **Hard no's:** no pay-to-apply, no per-action metered fees, no percentage of gig payouts, no
   weekly meters, no locked tiers, no "sign up to see the pay."

Pricing anchors from the data: the category's revealed per-season budget is ~$25-30 (gray-market
LinkedIn at $25/yr, Teal $29/mo bought for one sprint month). A Search Pass around $19-29 one-time
per season sits inside that. An Offer Pass at $49-99 flat with money-back is far below Levels.fyi's
$660 human service while landing at the moment the user just gained thousands.

## Traps to design around

- A glitchy pass is a brand-killer: this audience rages at gating mechanics that misfire even when
  it's a bug (DoorDash pass-reset threads). Passes must be bulletproof and self-serve.
- Aggregation alone has zero confirmed monetization signal: the one person who built a multi-site
  gig aggregator got silence, not "take my money." Fragmentation pain drives acquisition, not
  revenue. The wedge stays the offer moment plus depth.
- Matched betting has real paid-tool WTP (OddsMonkey £39.99/mo) but it's execution tooling, already
  owned, and a subscription. Not our lane.
